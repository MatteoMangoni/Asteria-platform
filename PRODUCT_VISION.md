# ASTERIA — Product Vision

## 1. Purpose

ASTERIA is an educational engineering project environment designed to help a student learn by carrying out a meaningful technical project with the support of an AI engineering mentor.

The first ASTERIA project is an asteroid rendezvous and exploration mission. The student investigates a real asteroid, develops models of the relevant physical system, builds and tests simulations, makes engineering decisions, and produces a coherent mission concept.

The mission itself provides the reason to learn the mathematics, physics, programming, data analysis and engineering reasoning needed to complete it.

ASTERIA is educational rather than flight-ready. Its models should be sufficiently simple to understand while remaining capable of answering meaningful engineering questions.

---

## 2. What ASTERIA Is

ASTERIA is **not primarily a chatbot, a simulator, an IDE, or a conventional educational course**.

It is an environment in which a student can take ownership of a substantial engineering project and progressively acquire the knowledge and skills required to complete it.

The project provides the context. The engineering problems create the need to learn. The AI provides guidance. The application preserves the project's state, work and accumulated evidence.

The first mission demonstrates this approach through a complete engineering problem:

**Earth → asteroid → rendezvous → science → mission review**

The student must understand not only whether a solution works, but why she has reason to believe that it works.

---

## 3. The Student's Role

The student is the **Mission Engineer**.

She owns the mission's reasoning, investigations, calculations, models, decisions and conclusions.

ASTERIA should therefore create the experience of being responsible for a real engineering problem rather than completing a sequence of predetermined exercises.

The student should progressively move from:

> "I don't know how to do this."

to:

> "I know what I need to find out."

then:

> "I found something."

and ultimately:

> "I understand why I believe it."

This progression is central to the intended mission experience.

---

## 4. Learning Through the Project

ASTERIA should not separate learning from doing.

The student encounters a mission question, discovers what she needs to understand, learns the relevant concepts, applies them, tests the result and records what she has learned.

The intended interaction pattern is:

**QUESTION → THINK → TRY → TEST → ASK → VERIFY → DOCUMENT**

The AI should support this process without removing the student's opportunity to reason and struggle productively.

The project should provide genuine reasons to learn mathematics, physics and programming rather than presenting those subjects as isolated lessons.

---

## 5. The Role of AI

The AI is the **Mission Engineering Mentor**.

It is a mentor, technical partner and research assistant — not the owner of the mission.

The AI should help the student:

- understand concepts;
- formulate useful questions;
- investigate unfamiliar topics;
- find and interpret authoritative information;
- choose appropriate methods;
- test assumptions;
- debug calculations and code;
- challenge reasoning;
- interpret results;
- recognise uncertainty and limitations;
- document important discoveries and decisions.

The AI should increase the student's capability rather than replace it.

Its preferred assistance follows an escalation ladder:

1. Question
2. Concept
3. Hint
4. Method
5. Small example
6. Implementation help
7. Complete solution when genuinely necessary

The objective is not to minimise the amount of work the student performs. It is to help her perform increasingly difficult work herself.

---

## 6. Protect Useful Struggle

ASTERIA should preserve the productive difficulty of engineering work.

The student should have opportunities to become stuck, form hypotheses, make mistakes, test ideas and discover solutions.

At the same time, frustration should not become the objective. When a problem becomes unproductive, the system should help reduce it into a smaller, more understandable problem rather than simply providing the answer.

The guiding principle is:

> **Protect the student's struggle, not the student's frustration.**

---

## 7. Engineering Reasoning

ASTERIA should make engineering reasoning visible.

The intended reasoning chain is:

**REQUIREMENT → ASSUMPTION → MODEL → INPUT → CALCULATION → RESULT → VALIDATION → UNCERTAINTY → CONCLUSION**

The student should learn to distinguish between:

> "I calculated this."

and:

> "I have good reason to believe this."

A numerical result produced by software is not automatically a reliable engineering result. The student should understand what a result means, which assumptions produced it, how it was validated, and what could make it wrong.

---

## 8. Models, Evidence and Uncertainty

ASTERIA should favour the simplest model capable of answering the current engineering question.

The first mission model deliberately simplifies the real system. For example, the initial mission may represent the Sun, Earth and asteroid as point masses and use simplified orbital and gravitational models. More complexity should be introduced when a mission question requires it.

The student should learn that simplification is an engineering decision, not automatically a flaw.

ASTERIA should also distinguish clearly between:

- known information;
- assumptions;
- estimates;
- calculations;
- simulation results;
- external data;
- uncertainty.

The system should never present an assumption as a fact or invent a result simply to keep the project moving.

---

## 9. The Project as the Organising Structure

The project is the primary unit of experience.

Knowledge is acquired because the project requires it. Investigations exist because questions need answering. Calculations and simulations exist because they help resolve those questions. Decisions are made because the mission requires choices. Results and evidence accumulate into the final engineering outcome.

The project therefore provides continuity across different activities and interactions.

The student's work should form a coherent body of engineering reasoning rather than a collection of disconnected conversations, exercises or code fragments.

---

## 10. Continuity and Mission Ownership

ASTERIA should maintain an authoritative representation of the project's state.

The AI should not be treated as the authoritative source of mission state. Important mission information should persist independently of any individual conversation or AI interaction.

This allows the student to move between different investigations and specialist contexts without losing the overall mission.

The human Mission Director remains the supervisor of the project. The AI supports the Mission Engineer within that structure.

---

## 11. Artifacts as Evidence of Thinking

The outcome of ASTERIA should not be only a conversation or a working program.

The project should progressively produce tangible engineering artifacts such as:

- calculations;
- models;
- Python programs;
- plots and visualisations;
- investigated data;
- assumptions;
- validation results;
- engineering decisions;
- mission designs;
- scientific findings;
- documented limitations.

These artifacts should provide evidence of the student's reasoning and allow the final mission to be explained and reviewed.

---

## 12. Engineering Authenticity

ASTERIA should feel like engineering.

Problems should be expressed naturally and concretely rather than as artificial educational exercises.

The student should encounter incomplete information, modelling choices, uncertainty, unexpected results and competing considerations.

The product should encourage questions such as:

- What are we actually trying to find out?
- What do we know?
- What are we assuming?
- What model is appropriate?
- How could we test this?
- Does the result make physical sense?
- What could explain the discrepancy?
- How confident are we?
- What decision follows from the evidence?

The objective is not merely to produce a technically functioning simulation. The student should be able to explain the physical ideas, mathematics, code, engineering decisions and limitations behind the mission.

---

## 13. The Experience Should Feel Like Engineering

ASTERIA should make substantial technical work feel meaningful and exciting.

The student should feel that she is responsible for a mission whose outcome depends on the quality of her reasoning.

Visualisations, simulations, mission status, spacecraft concepts and other product elements may contribute to this experience where they genuinely support the engineering work.

They should reinforce the feeling of being the engineer responsible for the mission, rather than turning the project into a game or disguising educational exercises as a game.

---

## 14. Student Autonomy as a Long-Term Outcome

A successful ASTERIA experience should leave the student more capable of beginning and completing the next project.

The desired trajectory is not increasing dependence on the AI.

It is increasing independence:

**AI-supported work → guided independent work → increasingly autonomous engineering**

The long-term value of ASTERIA therefore lies not only in the particular mission completed, but in the student's growing ability to investigate unfamiliar problems, learn what she needs, build solutions, test them and explain her conclusions.

---

## 15. Product Principles

The following principles guide the product:

1. **Student ownership**  
   The student owns the mission's reasoning and decisions.

2. **AI as mentor, not owner**  
   AI supports capability rather than replacing it.

3. **Learning through meaningful work**  
   The project creates the reason to learn.

4. **Protect useful struggle**  
   Difficulty is valuable when it remains productive.

5. **Understanding over automation**  
   A result is valuable when the student understands where it came from and why it can be trusted.

6. **Make reasoning visible**  
   Requirements, assumptions, models, evidence, validation and uncertainty should be distinguishable.

7. **Preserve continuity**  
   Important project knowledge should survive individual conversations and interactions.

8. **Treat failures as engineering history**  
   Unexpected results and unsuccessful approaches can contribute to understanding.

9. **Prefer appropriate simplicity**  
   Complexity should be justified by the question the project needs to answer.

10. **Increase autonomy**  
    The student's capability should grow faster than her dependence on the system.

11. **Make engineering feel meaningful**  
    The experience should create the feeling of working on a real technical problem.

---

## 16. Product North Star

The central question for future ASTERIA product decisions is:

> **Does this help the student become more capable of understanding, investigating and completing meaningful engineering projects herself?**

A second practical test should accompany it:

> **Does this make her feel more like the engineer responsible for the mission, or more like a student using an educational application?**

When those two questions conflict with convenience or feature complexity, the product should favour the student's long-term capability and ownership.

---

## 17. Scope of This Document

This document defines the **product vision and principles** of ASTERIA.

It does not yet define:

- detailed product requirements;
- the complete user interface;
- the detailed student workflow;
- the application architecture;
- the data model;
- AI system architecture;
- implementation technologies;
- hosting or deployment;
- the full set of future project templates.

Those decisions should be developed separately when there is sufficient evidence to make them.
